Design Principles¶

An Agentic App is a decision-making partner, not a data retrieval tool. That one idea shapes every builder choice: which agents to include, how sections are laid out, what prompts say, and which actions are offered. If you get it wrong, you end up with an expensive dashboard.

Measure every agent, prompt and widget against the four principles below.

Principle 1 — Proactive alerting over passive monitoring¶

Traditional systems wait for the user to look. In an Agentic App, agents analyze the data continuously and bring anomalies, risks and opportunities to the user as soon as the app opens.

Builder mandate

  • Bring issues to the user. Don't make users hunt for problems.
  • Give the alert context. Say what happened, why it matters and what can be done. An alert with no context causes anxiety, not action.
  • Classify urgency. Use the framework's priority levels (critical, high/warning, medium/info, success) consistently.
  • Respect attention. Being proactive does not mean being noisy. Show the right amount at the right time.

Example

Instead of making an HR manager run, sort and cross-reference an attrition report, the app opens with: "3 engineers show elevated attrition risk. Two cite career development; one has had no promotion in 28 months."

Principle 2 — Decision support, not data dumps¶

Users need the signal, not every row. Configure agents to synthesize, not to list.

  • Lead with the conclusion. The section headline is the agent's most important finding. Supporting data sits underneath.
  • Recommend and show trade-offs. Don't stop at the problem. Propose options.
  • Quantify impact. Write "12 invoices totaling $847K are overdue (6% of Q3 receivables)", not "several invoices are overdue".
  • Aggregate intelligently. Group lists by priority or category, and compare metrics to benchmarks or trends.

The 10-second synthesis test

Before publishing, look at the agent's initial display. If a person needs more than about 10 seconds to find the key takeaway, rewrite the display prompt so it leads with the insight.

Principle 3 — Action-oriented intelligence¶

Every time a user leaves the app to act on an insight, the work can stall. Agentic Apps close that gap by putting the means to act in the same place as the insight.

Action kind Behavior Example
Instant Completes in one click, then confirms and refreshes "Extend negotiation deadline by 7 days"
Workflow Multiple steps: review, confirm or collect inputs, then execute "Award negotiation to Supplier B"
Communication-triggering Tied to a high-priority item; produces a pre-drafted, record-specific message "Notify supplier of renewal terms"

The critical design question: Does this capability help the user make a decision or take an action? If it doesn't, a standard report or dashboard is a better fit.

Principle 4 — Context-aware prioritization¶

If all 47 items are marked "important", nothing is. Agents must understand the business goal and the user's role so they can rank by urgency and impact. For example, a $5M contract that expires in 3 days ranks above a $50K contract that expires in 30.

  • Use priority tiers consistently across cards, lists and actions.
  • Show the vital few in the Summary View. Expand in Section Focus and go deep in Item Focus (see Anatomy).
  • Shape output by role. Use $OraUserContext (see Context Variables) to tailor content to the person viewing it.
  • Collapse low-priority items. Visual hierarchy is a form of prioritization.

Anti-pattern

If the Summary View routinely shows more than 5–7 top-level items, the agents are not prioritizing hard enough. Tighten the prompts.

How the principles compound¶

flowchart LR
    P1["1 · Proactive alert<br/>'3 supplier contracts expire this week'"] --> P2["2 · Decision support<br/>'AcmeParts riskiest: SLA down 40%'"]
    P2 --> P3["3 · Action<br/>'Renew with 5% discount' + draft email"]
    P3 --> P4["4 · Prioritization<br/>Highest-value, soonest-expiring first"]

In one session the user goes from not knowing about the problem to having it resolved, without leaving the app.
